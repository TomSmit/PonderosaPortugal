## Who it is for

For young people who are stuck, in their room or behind a screen, sometimes in a home where things pinch. Often school stopped working too. Whoever comes here has to want to break out of something and have the strength for it, and that has little to do with age. So we talk with you at length before we decide together whether you come along.

## Setting off

At an agreed time you are dropped off at Eindhoven airport, where you join the group to fly to Portugal together. In Porto someone is waiting to drive you to Trinta. You will hear the dates well in advance.

## The first evening

After a day of travelling you arrive tired, and dinner is already on the table. After that you find your bed. The next day nothing is expected yet. You can wander around and see where you have ended up, and if you feel like it you help with the cooking.

## A day

In the morning we get up together and you make your own breakfast, usually bread from the village bakery with honey from the beekeeper. Then we go to work, though the work is different every day.

Sometimes you go with the beekeeper to spin honey or melt beeswax. Another time you carry the groceries down from the village or help in the vegetable garden, where there is always something to sow or harvest. Everyone cooks and everyone tidies, and in between there is plenty of time to do nothing at all.

The work is not there to keep you busy. People who have spent the day working with their hands often notice that their head is quieter in the evening.

## The village and beyond

Now and then there is a market in the village and you help behind the stall. If there is a party, you go along. We also go to Guarda, the town nearby, and take trips around the region. Six weeks is really too short for everything there is to do.

## What it does to you

In the first few days most young people think: where on earth have I ended up? Then it gets uncomfortable for a while, until calm slowly arrives and you even start to enjoy it. By the end most are sorry it is over. That is often how it goes, although everyone has their own pace and some stay in the discomfort longer than others.

That discomfort is part of it, and it helps if you open yourself up to it. The heat and the silence of the hills are bigger than you, and it takes a bit of courage to give yourself over to them.

## When it gets hard

There is a good chance that one day you will cry or want to go home. That is allowed, because the place simply does not leave you alone. We walk beside you then and make the step smaller, so you can take it. Taking it is up to you.

Anger, to be honest, does not worry us. It is often the first sign that something is starting to move.

Somewhere in those weeks you also notice that the group is counting on you. If you do not cook, there is no food on the table. Many young people find that a relief afterwards, because they simply belong and nobody keeps talking about them.

## Your phone

Your phone can come along and you can call home whenever you like. You will not do much else with it, because there is little electricity and no wifi. If we notice it getting between you and the group, we will tell you. Drugs and other substances are not welcome.

## What you take home

A jar of honey, at the very least. And the knowledge that you got through something you may have thought beforehand you could not do. Not everything that weighed on you is gone because of it, but you do have something to build on.
