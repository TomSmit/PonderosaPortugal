## You are already nature

The philosopher Timothy Morton says somewhere that you do not need to become ecological, because you already are. You are made of carbon, like the oak beside the house, and so you are as much a piece of nature as that tree. In a life lived mostly indoors, that is easy to forget.

Gregory Bateson spent his life searching for what he called the pattern which connects. At the quinta you come across that pattern without looking for it, for instance when you spread honey on your bread from the bees you saw the day before. Living like that for a few weeks, you start to see yourself a little less as the centre of everything. Most young people experience that as a relief rather than a loss.

## Doing

In 1967 the French educator Fernand Deligny moved with a few adults and autistic children to Monoblet, a village in the Cévennes. He distrusted the endless talk about children and the jargon that comes with it, and preferred to give them a rope to pull or wood to break. He did not call the adults around him educators. They lived close to the children without trying to change them, and that is how we try to do it at the quinta.

## Everyone carries the place

At La Borde, a French clinic founded by Jean Oury in 1953, where Félix Guattari worked for many years, residents helped keep the place running and work passed from hand to hand. At the quinta we do not need to organise that, because it follows from the circumstances. If nobody cooks, nobody eats, and if nobody takes out the rubbish bags, they are still there the next day.

Emmanuel Levinas wrote about the face of the other person, which addresses you before you know anything about them. Whoever sits at the table every evening with ten others is seen, and looks too. People lived like this for centuries, in communities where everyone was needed. We have partly lost that, and finding a few weeks of it again does more to you than we can properly explain.

## Nobody gets brought up here

The Dutch philosopher Jan Bransen calls the human being homo educandus, a creature that shapes itself. Jacques Rancière assumed that everyone's intelligence is equal, and that whoever learns something does not first need someone to explain it. So we do not come to the quinta as experts who are going to fix someone. We live there together for a while, and we learn at least as much from the young people as they learn from us.

## Art

Art belongs here because the mind wants to express itself. You do not need to be an artist for that, a song or a drawing can be enough. Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi described how you can be so absorbed in what you do that you forget time. At the quinta that happens just as easily while spinning honey as with a pencil in your hand.

## A summer inside you

> In the midst of winter, I found there was, within me, an invincible summer.
>
> Albert Camus

Setbacks are part of life, and resilience means being able to deal with them. Many of the young people who come to us do not yet stand firmly on their own feet. With every disappointment they retreat into their shell, looking for a safety they do not find there.

Living like that, you develop survival tricks that may have helped at the time but that later, as an adult, mostly get in the way. Rather than learning new tricks, we prefer to build solid ground together.

## Few rules

We hardly have any rules. Some things go without saying, like tidying up what you used and putting the food in the fridge on time. And if someone spends the whole afternoon slumped on the sofa, we simply say it is time to roll up their sleeves.

## Why Portugal

Mirjam, who started the Ponderosa, came to the Netherlands from Portugal and brought this way of living with her. In the Netherlands it does not happen by itself, and it often takes a long time for the penny to drop.

At the quinta we hardly need to explain it. No parcels are delivered and there will never be a dishwasher, so you really do need each other. Young people understand that there much faster than in the Netherlands.

It is not a spiritual place. We start from a humanist foundation, and what young people experience there often changes how they see themselves.
